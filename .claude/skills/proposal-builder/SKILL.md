# Proposal Builder SOP

## Before Writing
1. Read `context/offer_catalog.md` in full — this is the source of truth for
   every rate, tier, and term.
2. Read `context/business_info.md` → Standing Business Rules.
3. Check `clients/[name]/profile.md` or the client's Notion row, if either
   exists — never propose a number that contradicts a rate or status already
   on file for them.

## Structure
Always build a **three-tier ladder**, never a single number:
- Take the three tiers from `context/offer_catalog.md` → How to Present It,
  which has one preset for small studios and one for companies. The legacy
  client cards at the bottom of that file both lost and are not templates.
- Bold the middle tier and recommend it in one sentence. Close with one
  concrete question, ideally a date.
- Price follows what the client receives. A day rate is an occasional
  reference, not the basis of the quote.

## Pricing Rules (non-negotiable)
- Never discount the same deliverable twice.
- Never quote a stale high-volume/low per-unit rate (Sushi La ~€83/reel,
  Magic Island €50/reel, or any similar anchor) to a client it wasn't
  negotiated for.
- The **lowest tier is the concession point** — if a client needs a lower
  number, move them down a tier. Never reduce the top film price itself.
- No pre-discounting a new business before they've asked.
- Keep pricing consistent with what comparable clients have been quoted —
  the Cyprus market is small and people talk.

## Terms to Always Include
- What's included (camera/lenses/lighting).
- Number of revision rounds.
- Posting/usage rights (organic on client's channels with credit vs. paid ad
  usage, which is quoted separately).
- Payment split — typically 50% on booking, 50% on delivery.

## Voice
Match `context/brand_voice.md`: confident, no apology, no reference to old
prices, no emojis.
