# Virality SOP (Paddy Galloway playbook, adapted)

Source: "Open Residency x Paddy Galloway, The Virality Playbook" (PDF,
ep. 041). Paddy runs packaging and strategy for channels doing about 700M
views a month. Red Bull went from about 400K to over 9M average views per
video with him, and he says the gap was strategy, not budget or production.

## Read This First: What Virality Is For Here
Mykyta is a filmmaker who sells films. **Views are a means, not the goal.**
- **Every posting plan carries a baseline and a pass mark counted in
  enquiries or paid work, not reach.** That is a standing rule from the 4 Oct
  /court trial in `context/memory.md`. Write both down before posting.
- **Posting never replaces outreach.** Instagram has not yet brought a client
  on record. Skip the post, not the outreach.
- **Packaging must be honest.** The title and thumbnail open a question that
  the film actually answers. A promise the film does not keep kills retention,
  and YouTube stops recommending it. It also damages trust with the subject.
- **Subjects come first.** Never post anything beyond what the subject
  approved in writing. Every name, date and number in a public title, caption
  or thumbnail is checked against the record first. Never use a framing the
  subject has rejected; Welbry is never "closing" and John is never "the
  last" anything.
- Client-facing text follows `context/brand_voice.md`: no emojis, no em
  dashes, no double hyphens, no parentheses. Captions go through
  `caption-writer`.

## 1. Click, Watch, Satisfy, Repeat
The core model. Check every post against all four.
- **CLICK.** Title and thumbnail are about **50% of the result**. Paddy's team
  makes up to 50 thumbnails per video; most creators make one. For Mykyta:
  make **3 to 5 thumbnail concepts** minimum and run YouTube's **Test and
  Compare** on the best three.
- **WATCH.** Once someone clicks, keep them. The algorithm weights watch time
  and retention above nearly everything. The first 5 to 10 seconds must
  deliver on the title. Slow, silent cold opens belong to the festival cut,
  not the YouTube cut.
- **SATISFY.** The video must feel finished. Comments, likes and replays are
  rewarded, not just raw watch time. A real ending, like Welbry's last line
  with John's laugh, is a satisfaction signal.
- **REPEAT.** One fluke is not a business. Build a repeatable format, for
  example a series of maker films with the same structure and packaging
  logic.

## 2. The Idea Ceiling: audit before you shoot or post
"Why would most people in your niche watch this?" If the honest answer is
"they wouldn't, I just found it interesting", the idea has a low ceiling,
however well it is shot. **Polished production on a weak idea still gets
1,000 views.**
1. **Know the market size.** Craft and maker films are a mid-size niche. Set
   targets to match.
2. **Audit the ceiling.** Ask the question above honestly, before the shoot
   where possible.
3. **Expand the angle, not the niche.** Same subject, bigger framing. "Full
   guide to pond lining" got about 10K views; "I built a wildlife pond in my
   backyard" got 25 to 30M. For a maker film, frame the person and the turn
   in their story, not the technique. "Stained glass leading techniques"
   versus "His wife gave him a stained glass mirror. Now he calls it junk."
4. **Start narrow, then expand.** Never start broad and go narrow.
5. **Floor check.** The floor is what your worst video gets from loyal
   viewers. Everything above it is execution and packaging.

## 3. CCN: Core, Casual, New
Every video should work for all three audiences at once:
- **Core:** people who watch everything.
- **Casual:** people who catch one video in three or four.
- **New:** everyone in the market you have not reached yet.

Most creators build for one group and shut out the other two. In the
playbook's example, a video for core fans only got about 400K views, one for
core and casual got about 1M, and one for all three got 14M.

**New Viewer Transparency Rule:**
- Never say "as I said last time". New viewers were not there.
- Introduce every person in context, inside the film, never assumed.
- No video should need another one to make sense.
- **Test:** can someone who has never heard of Mykyta, John or Welbry watch it
  and fully enjoy it?

## 4. Outlier Ideation: find what already works
An **outlier** is a video at **3 to 4 times its channel's average views**.
Outliers are the signal; the tool used to find them does not matter. Paddy
uses View Stats, vidIQ and One of Ten.
1. **Internal:** what has already worked on your own channel? Do it again
   fast, before others copy it.
2. **External:** scan channels in your niche and adjacent ones for outliers
   from the last two years. For each, ask what your version would look like.
   Paddy's example: a golf channel studying fishing outliers found "fishing
   with wooden rods" and made "playing golf with wooden clubs". For Mykyta,
   study outliers on mini-documentary, craft, process and "people who make
   things" channels.
3. **Innovation:** combine two formats or niches that have not been put
   together before. Keep it to about 20% of uploads, not 100%.
4. **For a new channel:** start with external research, since there is no
   internal history. Bring in internal signals after 5 to 10 videos. Save
   innovation until there is data.

**The remix move,** from the Noah Kagan case: take the concept from one
outlier, the thumbnail layout from a second and the title structure from a
third, then combine them into something new. Kagan was averaging 5K to 10K
views; the remix got 5M.

## 5. The 100-10-1 Ideation System
You do not need better ideas; you need more of them and a way to cut. Paddy's
ratio is generate 500, filter to 50, present 5. **Mykyta's scale:** generate
about 30 to 50 post or film ideas, filter to 10, pick 1 to 3.

**Filters, in order:**
1. **Thumbnail test.** Can the idea be shown in one compelling image? If not,
   drop it. This one filter kills about two-thirds of ideas, because it is
   really a clarity test.
2. **80% overlap rule.** Would 80% of the people who watched the last post
   want this one too? Keep 80% of posts on-core and 20% for experiments.
   Adjacent-sounding topics can share almost no audience; golf and fishing
   share about 2%.
3. **CCN:** does it serve core, casual and new viewers?
4. **Executable:** can he actually make it with his kit, his budget and
   subject consent?
5. **Packageable:** can it be titled and thumbnailed cleanly?

## 6. The Glance Test: packaging as strategy
Viewers give a thumbnail about **one second**. They react; they do not read
or evaluate. If the idea needs 30 seconds to explain out loud, it will not
survive the scroll. Every video Paddy has seen pass 10M views can be said in
one second.

- **One-second rule:** is the premise clear from the thumbnail alone?
- **Log-line rule:** can the whole idea be one short sentence? If not, it is
  a loose thought, not an idea.
- **Unpause test:** does it feel like something the viewer needs to see?
- **Interruption test:** would someone about to do something important stop
  to watch this first?
- **Universal appeal check:** would a big YouTuber and someone's
  non-creator parent both find it interesting?

**Curiosity gap:** the title and thumbnail pose a question only the video
answers. Paddy's example is "Going to 5 NBA games in 50 hours": did he make
it, and how? Build the gap deliberately into both, ideally before shooting.

**If the idea cannot be put in one line anyone gets immediately, that is not
a packaging problem. It is not a good idea yet.**

## Packaging Checklist (run every time)
1. **Log-line** in one sentence.
2. **Title:** a specific person, a turn or contradiction, and one concrete
   detail like a number, place or object. Put the search term in the title or
   description, for example "stained glass" plus Limassol, Cyprus. Never use
   adjectives instead of facts.
3. **Thumbnail:** one subject, a readable face or strong colour, high
   contrast, and 0 to 3 words of text that add to the title rather than
   repeat it. Readable at phone size. Make 3 to 5 concepts and test three.
4. **First 5 to 10 seconds** deliver the title's promise. Lead with the
   strongest line or image.
5. **New viewer test** passes.
6. **Ending** feels finished, to earn the satisfaction signal.
7. **Vertical cut-downs,** 3 to 4 per film, each with its own hook, each
   pointing to the full film.
8. **Distribution:** list the communities that already care about this
   subject, such as the subject's own network, interest subreddits, Facebook
   groups and local press. Post as a story, never as an ad.
9. **Pass mark** written down before posting, in enquiries or paid work.
10. **Consent and facts:** within what the subject approved in writing, and
    every name, date and number checked.

## Worked example: Welbry, Oct 2026
- **Log-line:** a British soldier came to Cyprus in 1978, his wife gave him a
  stained glass mirror, and 39 years of glass later he calls that mirror
  junk.
- **Film title:** "Technician, Not an Artist". **YouTube title:** "His Wife
  Gave Him a Stained Glass Mirror. Now He Calls It Junk."
- **Thumbnail:** John lifting the finished piece to the window, his face in
  coloured light.
- **Shorts:** "money back in three hours", "piece of junk", "technician, not
  an artist", the snap of the glass.
- **Distribution:** John's students, British expats and the British Forces
  community in Cyprus, r/StainedGlass, and Cyprus Mail or In-Cyprus.
- **Pass mark:** at least 2 maker or small-business enquiries mentioning the
  film within 4 weeks.
- **Never:** "last", "closing" or "retiring".
